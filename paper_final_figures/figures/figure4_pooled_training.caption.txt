Dataset-specific random effects improve pooled probes on datasets with
non-standard prompts or domains. Results are shown for all 12 datasets,
prompts under True/False framing, and 88% depth layer per model. (a) AUC of
pooled L2 probes, the pooled difference in class means, domain- and
dataset-level random-slopes models, the shared direction of the random-slopes
fit on its own, and within-dataset L2 probes as a ceiling on performance. The
pooled probe sits within 0.009 of the median within-dataset AUC, but the gap
reaches 0.20 on Anatomy and 0.12 on VDS; using a dataset-level random slopes
model brings every gap below 0.05 and beats the pooled probe. The pooled
difference in class means, the direction that moves the model's answer in
Figure 6, trails pooled L2 by 0.05–0.06 AUC in the median and by up to 0.15
on WJB-adversarial, and beats it only on Gemma VDS (+0.08). (b) Signed
Mahalanobis cosine similarity between the pooled L2 direction or each
random-slopes component and the within-dataset L2 direction. Every inner
product is taken through the Ledoit-Wolf regularized covariance of the pooled
activations rather than in raw coordinates, so two coefficient vectors count
as aligned when they order the activations alike; signs are retained. The
shared random-slopes direction tracks pooled L2 closely (direct similarity
0.83 for Gemma and 0.87 for Qwen) and is moderately aligned with a typical
within-dataset direction, median 0.58 against 0.54 for pooled L2; dataset
deviations align better still at 0.79, and the shared direction plus the
dataset's own deviation reaches 0.83. Measured in raw coordinates the first
two medians fall to 0.26 and 0.19 while the deviations are unchanged at 0.78,
so most of the apparent disagreement between the pooled and within-dataset
directions lies along directions the activations barely vary in.
