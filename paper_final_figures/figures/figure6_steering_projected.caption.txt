Classifier weights restricted to the leading principal components of their
training data, used as steering directions. We steer last-token activations
at 88% depth on six true/false framed datasets with unit-length vectors; a
step of 1 equals the dataset's class-mean separation, and the vertical axis
is the resulting shift in True-versus-False log odds per step. Each
probe weight vector (L2-regularized and random-slopes probes fitted on the
target, on the pool, and on the domain-absent pool, the latter dashed) is
projected onto the top 2, 10 or 50 principal components of the rows it was
fitted on.
Horizontal lines mark the difference-in-means directions on the target
and on the domain-absent pool; bands show random vectors after the same
projection onto the target's components (dark) and the domain-absent
pool's components (light), median of 8 draws. Each panel reports the
baseline model output AUC. With two components the probe weights steer as
strongly as the difference in means, but random vectors in the same plane
steer nearly as strongly, since a dataset's leading component is its class
separation. With ten components the pool and domain-absent L2 probes still
match the difference-in-means directions and stand clear of the noise band,
whereas the target-trained probe loses much of its effect and the
random-slopes probes are inconsistent. With fifty components every probe
direction falls toward the noise band: the steering content of a fitted
probe is confined to the few leading components of its training data and is
diluted by the many further components it also weights.
