Activation geometry predicts transfer within a domain but not across domains,
and without labels only the subspace measure does so well. (a) Spearman
correlation across source–target dataset pairs between geometric similarity
measures and AUCs of L2 probes trained on the source and tested on the target
(median and interquartile range over 16 model × framing × depth settings). The
probe-direction cosine, which needs the target's labels, ranks transfer almost
perfectly (0.87–0.96). The two label-free similarity measures (overlap of the
datasets' 20 top principal components, and covariance whitened distance
between their mean activations) rank transfer performance moderately and
weakly within domains (0.59 and 0.12) and not at all across them (0.05 and
0.00). (b) Alignment of two datasets' probe directions by how close the
centroids of the source and target dataset are: same-domain alignment falls
from 0.56 to 0.28 with distance, while different-domain pairs are not
dependent on distance (0.23–0.29).
